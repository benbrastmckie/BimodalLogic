6. **The representation-theorem strategic bet: lead with algebraic, not Kamp, and not Jónsson–Tarski as currently scoped.** Three routes exist in the codebase; they are not equally positioned:
   - **Kamp-style expressive completeness** is *already fully exploited* — the Kamp chain (`kamp_prior_expressive_completeness`, `US_expressively_complete_over_prior`) is complete and sorry-free, and it is used as a *tool inside* discrete weak completeness (§6 of the document), not as a representation-theorem route in its own right. Presenting it in §7 as a forward-looking "direction" would misstate its actual role; it belongs in §6 (where the document already puts it) and should not compete for space in §7.
   - **Jónsson–Tarski / algebraic duality** is the one candidate with *live, sorry-free groundwork already in the tree*: `Metalogic/Algebraic/` (Lindenbaum–Tarski algebra, ultrafilters, interior operators) is measured sorry-free today. The document currently buries this fact in §7's middle paragraph, after a "Superseded Waypoint" preamble about a defective draft that contributes nothing forward-looking. The live algebraic asset should open §7, not follow throat-clearing about what was wrong with an old draft.
   - **Shift-set / ultraproduct** is the roadmap's officially sanctioned next gate (task 424) precisely because it is cheaper to attempt first, but it has zero live code and an unresolved carrier-universe risk the design document itself flags as an open engineering question, not a mathematical one.

   The strategic recommendation: commit the document's rhetorical weight to the **algebraic route**, for three independent reasons that reinforce each other and that I'd argue for stating explicitly rather than leaving implicit: (a) it is the only route with a nonzero, verified Lean asset today, so it is the lowest-risk claim to make to a reader who will judge credibility partly by what is actually built; (b) it is squarely inside Scott's own methodological home turf (Boolean-valued models, Stone-type duality, domain theory are all algebraic-representation techniques), so it is the framing most likely to produce a substantive response rather than a polite one; (c) the shift-set route's payoff (first-order axiomatizability, compactness) is explicitly gated behind task 424's feasibility check *before* the expensive work is authorized — so touting it as the leading direction overstates its current standing relative to what the roadmap has actually committed to.

7. **The Scott opportunity — the single best question to pose, and why it is not generic flattery.** `FormalFoundations.typ:378` already names the exact open technical obstruction blocking algebraic representation: *"Of `def:frame`'s four axioms, Spherical is the prime suspect — hardest to discharge at infinite carriers, exactly where the Jönsson–Tarski route stumbles."* Spherical is a directed-family / filter-base intersection condition (`⋂ 𝒮 ≠ ∅` for directed families of nonempty fibers and segments) — structurally a neighbourhood-filter condition, not a point-relational one. This is precisely the kind of structure Scott–Montague neighbourhood semantics was built to handle by replacing a single accessibility relation with families of neighbourhoods/filters at each point, generalizing Kripke semantics exactly along the axis (relational vs. filter-based) where the task-frame construction is currently stuck. Posing the question directly — *does Spherical admit a neighbourhood-semantic reformulation that survives infinite carriers, and does that reformulation still support a Jónsson–Tarski-style canonical embedding?* — hands Scott a question that (i) is genuinely open, (ii) sits exactly on his own foundational contribution, and (iii) is stated at a level of formal precision (the actual axiom, the actual document location, `def:frame`) that lets him engage immediately rather than needing to reconstruct the setup. This is the strongest, most concrete version of "what's worth putting in front of this reader" available in the current materials, and it should anchor the close of §7 rather than being one open item among several.

   A secondary, lower-priority hook: Prior's *tomorrow/yesterday* operators are credited by Prior himself (and cited in `possible_worlds.tex:1308`) to a remark of Scott's — the discrete-frame Next/Previous operators the document already defines (§1) trace their philosophical lineage to Scott directly. This is worth one sentence, not a section: it is a nice personal touch but has no open-problem content, unlike the Spherical/neighbourhood question.
