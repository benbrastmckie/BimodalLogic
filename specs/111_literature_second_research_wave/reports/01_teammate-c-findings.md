Without this content, the literature collection is missing its best modern survey of completeness techniques for temporal logic over arbitrary linear orders.

**Recommended action**: Obtain via university library (Elsevier institutional access) or via interlibrary loan. The chapter is 66 pages (pp. 655–720 in the printed volume).

### 5. Major Missing Domain: The Bimodal/Combination Literature

The most significant blind spot in the current literature collection is the **combinations-of-modal-logics literature**, particularly:

**Thomason (1984), "Combinations of Tense and Modality"** — This chapter appears in the *same volume* as Burgess 1984 (Handbook of Philosophical Logic Vol. II, pp. 135–165), yet is absent from the literature collection entirely. Thomason introduced the T×W framework for combining tense and modal logic, and also introduced the concept of "fusion" (independent combination of modal systems). The T×W logic is semantically a bimodal combination of a temporal accessibility relation with a modal (necessity) relation, directly analogous to BX. This is arguably the most relevant single paper the collection is missing.

**Gabbay and Shehtman (1998, 2000, 2002), "Products of Modal Logics" (Parts 1–3)** — This series, culminating in Part 3 ("Products of Modal and Temporal Logics," *Studia Logica* 72, 2002), directly addresses the decidability and completeness properties of logics combining S5-type modalities with temporal operators. Products of transitive modal logics are the algebraic/frame-semantic framework most natural for BX. The negative results (many products are Kripke-incomplete; undecidability in dimension ≥ 3) are important context for understanding why the Burgess/Xu approach (working directly with linear-order structures, not product frames) is the right strategy.

**Kurucz, Wolter, Zakharyaschev, Gabbay (2003), *Many-Dimensional Modal Logics: Theory and Applications*** — This Elsevier monograph (Studies in Logic, Vol. 148) gives the definitive treatment of two-dimensional logics combining S5-type modal logic with temporal or other logics. It would directly inform whether BX's completeness is obtainable by standard product methods or requires the Burgess-style chain construction. It is not in the literature collection.

### 6. Missing Domain: Formal Verification of Completeness Proofs

The literature collection contains no sources from the theorem-prover formalization tradition. This matters because the project's goal is a *Lean 4 formalization*, and there are important prior formalizations to learn from:

**Obendrauf, Baanen, Koopmann, Stebletsova (ITP 2024), "Lean Formalization of Completeness Proof for Coalition Logic with Common Knowledge"** — Published at ITP 2024, this ~6,000-line Lean 4 formalization proves completeness of Coalition Logic (with common knowledge extensions) using a canonical model construction. The paper uses Lean 4 type classes to generalize the canonical model, enabling proof reuse across related logics (CL, CLK, CLC). Directly relevant to BX because: (a) it demonstrates how to structure the Lean 4 canonical model infrastructure, (b) the truth lemma proof technique is the same Henkin-style argument needed for BX, and (c) the type-class generalization strategy may apply to BX's two-modal structure.

**FormalizedFormalLogic/Foundation (GitHub, active)** — An actively maintained Lean 4 project formalizing modal logic (Kripke and neighborhood semantics, Gödel-McKinsey-Tarski theorem). While it does not formalize temporal or bimodal logic, the infrastructure patterns for modal completeness proofs (MCS construction, accessibility relation encoding) are directly applicable.

**Ben-Ari (2021), "A Henkin-Style Completeness Proof for the Modal Logic S5"** — A Lean 3 formalization of S5 completeness, available on PhilArchive and as a Springer chapter (LNCS 13095). The canonical model for S5 uses equivalence classes of MCS (since every world accesses every world in S5). BX adds temporal structure on top of S5; this formalization shows how S5's simpler accessibility conditions are handled in Lean, which is the modal fragment BX inherits.

### 7. Missing Domain: S5 Completeness Specifically

The literature collection contains no source focused specifically on S5 completeness. The collection's tense-logic sources (Burgess, Verbrugge, Reynolds) all handle the temporal fragment but treat the modal (box) operator only incidentally. BX is a *bimodal* logic — temporal + S5 modal — and the completeness proof must jointly handle both operators.

The S5 completeness proof is standard (Henkin, 1949; treated in any modal logic textbook) but its interaction with temporal operators in a bimodal setting requires care. Specifically: in the canonical model for BX, the accessibility relation for the modal operator □ must be constructed consistently with the temporal chain structure. Burgess 1982 and Xu 1988 handle this in the bimodal case, but the collection has no source that explains the S5-specific subtleties in isolation.

**Blackburn, de Rijke, Venema (2001), *Modal Logic*** — The standard modern reference for modal logic. Chapter 4 ("Completeness") covers canonical models for normal modal logics including S5 in detail, and Chapter 7 ("Extended Modal Logic") covers multi-modal systems and fusion/product constructions. This textbook is not in the collection and is freely available in paperback from Cambridge.

### 8. Potential False Economy: Hirsch-Hodkinson 1997

The README lists Hirsch-Hodkinson 1997 with MEDIUM relevance for "game-theoretic model construction." Having verified the PostScript file exists, the substantive question is whether the paper actually helps with BX. The paper addresses **cylindric algebra representations** — specifically, the *step-by-step* construction of representations for cylindric and relation algebras using an Ehrenfeucht-Fraïssé style pebble game. This is several levels of abstraction removed from BX's chain construction.

The relevance claimed in the README ("game-theoretic model construction; structural insights") is tenuous. The Verbrugge 2004 step-by-step insertion technique (already obtained) is a direct method in the tense logic setting. Hirsch-Hodkinson 1997 is the abstract algebraic analog of a quite different approach. **The priority of obtaining/reading Hirsch-Hodkinson 1997 should be downgraded from MEDIUM to LOW compared to the missing sources identified above.**

### Domain Blind Spots

1. **Theorem-prover formalization literature** — entirely absent. The project IS a Lean 4 formalization; understanding how peers have handled similar structures (canonical models, truth lemmas, maximal consistent sets) in Lean would save significant implementation time.

2. **Products/combinations of modal logics** — entirely absent. BX is a combination of S5 modal logic with linear tense logic. The existence, difficulty, and known results for such combinations (including Kripke incompleteness of certain products) provide essential context for why the Burgess chain approach is the correct strategy.

3. **S5 completeness specifically** — treated incidentally at best. The modal fragment of BX (S5) has a well-understood Henkin completeness proof; the collection has no source focused on this fragment in isolation.

## Summary Recommendation

The literature collection has **four priority actions**:

1. **Obtain Hodkinson-Reynolds 2006 full chapter** — the single most useful survey source for completeness techniques, currently useless as a truncated 3-page stub. University library access is the realistic route.

2. **Obtain Xu 1988** — the only tracked source directly addressing bimodal S5+tense completeness on linear orders (the exact subject of BX). Institutional Springer access or author contact.

3. **Add Thomason 1984** — already behind the same paywall as Burgess 1984 (same Reidel handbook volume), so can be obtained simultaneously. Directly addresses the bimodal tense+modal combination.

4. **Add ITP 2024 (Obendrauf et al.)** — freely available at Dagstuhl DROPS. Provides concrete Lean 4 infrastructure patterns for canonical-model completeness proofs.

The Hirsch-Hodkinson 1997 paper, while accessible, should be deprioritized. Its algebraic content (cylindric algebra representations) is at best distant background material; the Verbrugge 2004 step-by-step technique already in the collection is the directly applicable source.
