### Logic Findings

#### 1. The elimination theorem: clause (a) is subsumed by clause (b) of the same automaton

**Theorem 1 (derived).** Let `v ∈ Ĉ^ω` be a word whose first letter certifies at least one past-type, i.e. `∃ pt ⊆ PastLits` with `⟐δ_pt ∈ v_0`. Then `v ∈ Viol⁺(a) ⟹ v ∈ Viol⁺(b)`. Dually, let `v` be a backward word whose first letter certifies at least one future-type; then `v ∈ Viol⁻(a) ⟹ v ∈ Viol⁻(b)`.

*Proof.* Suppose `Viol⁺(a)` fires: `⟐δ_{ft_lin(v)} ∉ v_0`. Set `γ := δ_{ft_lin(v)}`.

1. `γ ∈ cl(φ)`. Finding 1.3 puts `{δ_ft : ft ⊆ FutLits}` into `cl(φ)` outright.
2. `γ` is pure-future. `γ` is a conjunction of members of `FutLits` and their negations; each member of `FutLits` is a future component of a `sepZ` clause, hence `IsPureFuture` (compiled: `sepZ_sepClauses`'s second projection goes through `futDet_of_isPureFuture (isPureFuture_toPlus …)`, `Discharge.lean:110-117`); and `IsPureFuture` is closed under `imp` and has `bot`, so it is closed under negation and conjunction (compiled, `Formula.lean:309-316`).
3. `γ̄` is a pure-future *linear* formula. This is Finding 3.1's Lemma 3.1: the bar-translation sends `IsPureFuture` closure formulas to linear formulas with no `S` outside a letter, because the translation is componentwise on `atom`/`bot`/`imp`, sends the `box`/`stab` leaves to letters, and sends `untl` to `U`.
4. Therefore the truth of `γ̄` at position `0` is a condition on `v` alone. A pure-future linear formula at position `0` is built from letters by Booleans, `X` and `U`, so its evaluation consults `v_0, v_1, …` and **no pure-past subformula value at all**. In particular it does not read the clause-(b) device's initial transducer state `Π_0`, which is the only thing the hypothetical index `pt` controls (Finding 5.1).
5. `γ̄` is true at position `0` of `v`. By construction: `γ̄ = ⋀_{β ∈ ft_lin(v)} β̄ ∧ ⋀_{β ∈ FutLits \ ft_lin(v)} ¬β̄`, and `ft_lin(v)` is defined as exactly the set of `β` whose `β̄` holds at `0`.

Pick the certified `pt`. By (4) and (5), `γ̄` is true at position `0` of the bi-infinite word whose forward half is `v` and whose cut past-type is `pt`, whatever that `pt` is; and `⟐γ ∉ v_0` by hypothesis. That is a forward violation at `i = 0` under `pt` (Finding 5.1's definition, whose index range is `i ≥ 0` and whose `γ` ranges over all of `cl(φ)`). So `v ∈ Viol⁺_pt` and clause (b) fires. ∎

The mirror is the `reflectTime` image: `δ_{pt_lin(v)}` is pure-past (`IsPurePast` is likewise closed under `imp` and has `bot`, compiled `Formula.lean:318-325`), so `δ̄_{pt_lin(v)}` is a pure-past linear formula, so its truth at position `0` of the backward word is a condition on `(v_0, v_{-1}, …)` alone and does not read the mirror transducer's initial state, which is the only thing the hypothetical `ft` controls.

**Why this is the right lever.** The source report's own Claim 1⁺ already observes, parenthetically, that "clause (a) is the instance `γ = δ_{ft_lin(w)}` at `i = 0`" of badness. What it does not observe is that this instance is *inside clause (b) of the same automaton*, and the reason it is not obvious is that clause (b) evaluates `γ̄` under a hypothetical cut type that may be wrong. Step (4) is the whole content of the elimination: for a `γ` pure in the automaton's own reading direction, the position-`0` evaluation is blind to the hypothetical type, so a wrong-but-certified type is still a usable ticket.

**Strictness.** The inclusion is conditional and the languages genuinely differ: on a word whose first letter certifies nothing of the relevant polarity, `Viol⁺(b)` is empty while `Viol⁺(a)` may fire, so `Viol⁺(b) ⊊ Viol⁺`. Both consumption sites are unaffected, for different reasons (Finding 3 for the model direction, Finding 3's last paragraph for the consistency direction).

#### 2. The seed: every chronicle letter certifies a type of each polarity, from K1 alone

**Lemma 2 (derived).** Let `t` be a node of a two-sided chronicle and `h := λ(t)`. Put `pt_h := {α ∈ PastLits : α ∈ h}` and `ft_h := {β ∈ FutLits : β ∈ h}`. Then `⟐δ_{pt_h} ∈ h` and `⟐δ_{ft_h} ∈ h`, hence `⟐δ_{pt_h}, ⟐δ_{ft_h} ∈ ĉ(λ*(t))`.

*Proof.* Four steps, for `ft_h`; `pt_h` is identical.

1. **`h` decides every `β ∈ FutLits`.** `h = h(a,c)` for some `a ∈ c` (Finding 2.1), and `h(a,c) ⊇ {δ : δ ∈ a} ∪ {¬δ : δ ∈ fcl(φ⁺) \ a}`, so every member of `fcl(φ⁺)` occurs in `h` positively or negatively. And `FutLits ⊆ cl₀(φ) ⊆ Sub(φ⁺) ⊆ fcl(φ⁺)`: each `β ∈ FutLits` has `⟐β ∈ ecl(φ)` by Finding 1.3, each `⟐β` occurs as a conjunct of some `a_i = γ̂_{ĉ_i}` (Lemma 2.6), and each `a_i` occurs inside `θ_f`, a conjunct of `φ⁺` (Finding 6). So `β ∈ Sub(φ⁺)`.
2. **`⋀h ⊢ δ_{ft_h}`.** By (1), `h` contains `β` for `β ∈ ft_h` and `¬β` for `β ∈ FutLits \ ft_h`; `δ_{ft_h}` is precisely that conjunction.
3. **`⊢ δ_{ft_h} → ⟐δ_{ft_h}`.** This is the contrapositive of `stab_t` (`⊡φ → φ`, compiled, `Axioms.lean:288`) at `φ := ¬δ_{ft_h}`, since `⟐ψ = ¬⊡¬ψ`.
4. **`⟐δ_{ft_h} ∈ h`.** `δ_{ft_h} ∈ Sub(φ⁺)` (it is a subformula of `⟐δ_{ft_h} = ¬⊡¬δ_{ft_h}`, which occurs in `ecl(φ)` hence in `Sub(φ⁺)` by the chain in (1)), so `⟐δ_{ft_h} ∈ fcl(φ⁺)` and `h` decides it by (1). If `¬⟐δ_{ft_h} ∈ h` then by (2) and (3) `h` is inconsistent, contradicting **K1**. So `⟐δ_{ft_h} ∈ h`.

Finally `⟐δ_{ft_h}` is a `⟐`-formula, hence colour-decided (Lemma 2.2, which runs on `stab_4`/`stab_5`), so it lies in the letter `ĉ(λ*(t))` under that letter's second characterisation in Lemma 2.6. ∎

Three things are worth saying about what this lemma does and does not use. It uses **K1 and nothing else** from the chronicle clause list — not K3A/K3B, not the K4/K5 ban clauses, not the level split. It does **not** use the truth lemma, so it is available inside the truth lemma's induction with no circularity. And it does **not** claim `ft_h` is the walk's future-type; for an emergent walk it generally is not, and the elimination does not need it to be.

#### 3. The replacement for Step 3 of Finding 14.4

**Step 3′ (derived).** With both clause (a)s dropped, let `b` be a walk through the anchor class in the quotient and `w := w(b)`.

1. By Corollary 13.3 and Step 2 (both unchanged), `A⁺` does not accept `(w_0, w_1, …)` and `A⁻` does not accept `(w_0, w_{-1}, …)`.
2. By Lemma 2 at any `t ∈ b_0`, `w_0` certifies `ft_0 := ft_{λ(t)}`.
3. **The real past-type is certified.** Non-acceptance by `A⁻` means clause (b) does not fire, so in particular there is no backward violation under the certified `ft_0`. Instantiate it at position `0` with `γ := δ_{pt_lin(w)} ∈ cl(φ)`: by Theorem 1's mirror, `γ̄` is true at `0` and its truth there does not depend on `ft_0`. Non-violation therefore forces `⟐δ_{pt_lin(w)} ∈ w_0`.
4. **Forward badness-freedom, and the real future-type certified.** Non-acceptance by `A⁺` with the now-certified `pt_lin(w)` gives: for every `γ ∈ cl(φ)` and `i ≥ 0`, if `γ̄` holds at `i` then `⟐γ ∈ w_i`. Since `pt_lin(w)` is the word's *actual* linear past-type, Finding 5.1's transducer computes the actual running past-type, so this is the real-word condition. Instantiating it at position `0` with `γ := δ_{ft_lin(w)}` additionally gives `⟐δ_{ft_lin(w)} ∈ w_0`.
5. **Backward badness-freedom.** Non-acceptance by `A⁻` with the now-certified `ft_lin(w)` gives the same for every `i ≤ 0`.
6. Hence `w ⊨ χ̄₀`. Apply at `i := lvl t`, `γ := δ`, using Step 1 (unchanged) to get `w, lvl t ⊨ δ̄`; conclude `⟐δ ∈ w_{lvl t}`, i.e. `⟐δ ∈ λ(t)` by Lemma 2.2. ∎

The structural comparison with the source report's Step 3: there, two certifications are read off two clause (a)s in parallel and the two clause (b)s are then applied. Here, one certification is bootstrapped from K1 and the remaining three facts fall out of the two clause (b)s in a chain. The bookkeeping is one link longer; the automata are one disjunct smaller each; the parenthetical remark on `cl(φ⁺) \ cl(φ)` at the end of Finding 14.4 is untouched, since it never mentioned the clauses.

**The consistency direction needs no seed.** Claim 1⁺ (Finding 7.2) asserts an implication whose antecedent is automaton *acceptance*. Dropping a disjunct shrinks `Viol⁺`, hence shrinks the accepted language, hence strengthens the antecedent: the implication is preserved, and its proof simply loses the clause-(a) branch of its "clause (a) or clause (b) of `Viol⁺` therefore fires" case split. No seed, and no new obligation. (But see Finding 6 (D2): the branch that is lost was the only one establishing the consequent as stated, which is how the elimination exposes an existing mismatch rather than creating one.)

#### 4. The bi-infinite-automaton alternative, and why it is dominated

The dispatch's third admissible answer. Two claims, at different evidence tiers.

**(i) Derived, from the rule inventory.** The Auxiliary-Atoms rule is the only device that puts an automaton inside L⁺, and its antecedent `aaAnte b₀ Q k a p = b₀ ∧ ⊡G≤ ⋀_{b,i}((b ∧ a_i) → ⊡X p(b,i)) ∧ ⊡G≤ ⋀_{b ≠ b'} ¬(b ∧ b')` names a run **in one direction**: an initial-state atom at the evaluation point and a step clause propagated along `⊡G≤`. `ExtDerivation`'s rule set offers this and its `reflectTime` mirror (`⊡H≤`), and nothing else (read, the source report's Findings 6 and 7.5, citing the probe's eleven constructors). A bi-infinite automaton has no distinguished starting position, so there is no single fresh-atom family that names its run: naming one requires picking a cut and running outward in both directions — which is the two-sided pair already in place, with the cut supplied by `clockAnte q`. So the hinge is **forced by the rule inventory**, not a workaround for a missing automata-theoretic result. This argument is independent of whatever bi-infinite recognizability theory supplies.

**(ii) Recalled, not held.** The standard treatment of recognizable sets of bi-infinite words proceeds by exactly this cut-and-split: a bi-infinite word is recognized by taking a forward ω-automaton on the suffix, a backward ω-automaton on the reversed prefix, and a compatibility condition linking their cut states. Under that reduction a bi-infinite automaton *is* the present design, so it buys no simplification; and determinization with Rabin-shaped acceptance for the bi-infinite case is not available in the off-the-shelf form the ban mechanism consumes. **The Literature corpus was swept this session and holds no substantive source for this** — `grep -ril` over `~/Projects/Literature/sources/` returns only bibliography chunks, and a targeted `grep -n -i` over the one plausible candidate (`thomas_1997_languages/Thomas_1997_Languages_Automata_Logic.md`) returns bibliography lines only. This claim is therefore recalled, and (i) is what the verdict rests on.

**Verdict.** Not pursued, with reason. (i) alone suffices: even a perfect bi-infinite automaton could not be named by the rule set, so the alternative is not merely more expensive but unusable. Recording this closes the third branch of (Q3) rather than leaving it open.

#### 6. Two incidental defects located in the source report

Both are independent of (Q3), both were found by working through the clause-(b) semantics the elimination relies on, and both are made unavoidable by the elimination. Recorded here because the source report is the architecture of record.

**(D1) `PastLits`/`FutLits` must be closed under same-polarity subformulas of the translations.** Finding 5.1 states that the running past-type `Π_i ⊆ Λ⁻` — where `Λ⁻` is "the (finite) set of pure-past linear subformulas of the `ᾱ` for `α ∈ PastLits`, closed under subformulas" — has `Π_0` "determined by `pt` and `v_0`". But `pt ⊆ PastLits` records only which *top-level* `ᾱ` hold at `0`, while `Λ⁻` is explicitly subformula-closed, and Finding 1.1 defines `PastLits(X) := ⋃_{ψ ∈ X} {c.1 : c ∈ sepZ ψ}` — the top-level first components only. `sepZ`'s past components are `toPlus (leafVal φ) c.1` for `c.1` an S-only abstract formula (compiled, `Discharge.lean:93-117`), and their own pure-past subformulas need not themselves be clause components of anything in the closure. So `Π_0` is **not** determined by `pt` as defined, and the transducer has no well-defined initial state. *Repair*: in Finding 1.1, replace `PastLits(X)` by its closure under the pure-past subformulas of its members, and `FutLits(X)` dually. Both stay finite (a finite set's subformula closure is finite), and every downstream use is unaffected: Lemma 4.1's `PT(c₀) = {a ∩ PastLits : a ∈ c₀}`, Lemma 4.2's full product, Lemma 4.3's hue-recovery and Finding 4.4's letter content all quantify over whatever `PastLits` is. The enlargement is free in the only sense that matters (Risk: "the alphabet is enormous … only finiteness is load-bearing").
Note that Theorem 1 above does **not** depend on this repair: a pure-future `γ̄` reads no pure-past subformula at all, so step (4) of its proof never consults `Π_0`.

**(D2) Claim 1⁺'s consequent does not match the theorem Claim 2⁺ consumes.** Claim 1⁺ (Finding 7.2) concludes `¬χ̄₀⁺` with `χ̄₀⁺ := ⋀_{γ ∈ cl(φ)} G≤(γ̄ → ⟐γ)` — the *real-word* forward bad-word formula. Its proof derives, from acceptance, that `w ∈ Viol⁺`; and on the clause-(b) branch what that yields is a violation in the **hypothetical** word whose cut past-type is some certified `pt`, which need not be `pt_lin(w)`. So the proof's "in either case some `γ ∈ cl(φ)` has `γ̄` true at some `i ≥ 0` with `⟐γ ∉ w_i`" does not follow on the clause-(b) branch. Correspondingly Claim 2⁺ (Finding 7.4) cites Lemma 7.3's **unconditional** form (`⊢ χ̄₀⁺[⟐·]`), whereas the clause-(b) branch needs its **conditional** form, `⊢ ⟐δ_pt → ⊡G≤(γ̄^{pt}[⟐·] → ⟐γ)`, which Finding 7.3 proves separately and labels consumption point (S2) — and which Finding 4.3 describes as exactly "the theorem that licences running the forward automaton on *every* certified `pt`, not only on the walk's own". *Repair*: restate Claim 1⁺'s consequent as the negation of the conditional conjunction, `¬⋀_{pt ⊆ PastLits}(⟐δ_pt → ⋀_{γ ∈ cl(φ)} G≤(γ̄^{pt} → ⟐γ))` read at `0`, and have Claim 2⁺ consume Lemma 7.3's conditional form. The relation to (Q3): clause (a)'s branch *was* the one branch producing a real-word violation at `i = 0`, so its presence masked (D2) on half the case split. ### Math Findings

#### 8. The ω-regularity accounting, and why one site needs a seed and the other does not

The asymmetry between the two consumption sites is worth stating as a general fact about dropping a disjunct from a recognized language, because it is the reason the elimination is safe without any case analysis.

`Viol⁺(b) ⊆ Viol⁺(a) ∪ Viol⁺(b) = Viol⁺`, so `A⁺`'s language strictly shrinks. A shrinking accepted language has opposite effects at the two sites:

- **The consistency site (Claim 1⁺) consumes acceptance as an antecedent.** `acceptance → ¬badness`. Shrinking the language weakens the antecedent's reach, so the implication is preserved automatically, with no side condition. This is why Finding 3's last paragraph needs no seed.
- **The model site (Step 3) consumes non-acceptance as a hypothesis.** `¬acceptance → certification ∧ badness-freedom`. Shrinking the language *weakens* the hypothesis's consequences, so something must be recovered — and the recovery is precisely the seed of Lemma 2, which is why the seed is needed there and only there.

The quantitative content: each automaton's language was presented as a union of two ω-regular sets with two separate regularity arguments (Lemma 5.2 for clause (b), a parenthetical for clause (a)); after the elimination each is a single ω-regular set with one argument. The source report's own Risk entry on Lemma 5.2 ("an ω-regularity argument … at the level of 'LTL conditions are ω-regular', not a construction") therefore has one fewer instance to cover. No cardinality anywhere changes: `|Ĉ| = 2^{|ecl φ|}` is unchanged (Finding 5), and the state counts `|Q⁺|`, `|Q⁻|`, `K⁺`, `K⁻` are unconstrained by the paper proof in either version.

### Cross-Domain Synthesis

The logical and the combinatorial content meet at exactly one place in this question, and it is the same seam the source report identified: **the cut**.

What the source report's Cross-Domain Synthesis says of the cut is that free pasting at a state pays for itself three times — as the full product of types (order-theoretic), as derivable conditional badness (logical), and as the free down-and-across construction (combinatorial) — with separation the other half of the seam, making the cut index finite and state-level. The present finding adds a fourth payment, and it is the cheapest of the four: at the cut, and only at the cut, a *characteristic conjunction of same-direction-pure literals* has a truth value that the opposite direction cannot see. That is a purity fact, proved from the `IsPurePast`/`IsPureFuture` constructor lists (compiled) and transported to the linear side by the bar-translation's purity lemma. The clause-(a) cross-certification was built to carry information across the cut; what the elimination shows is that at the cut there is nothing to carry, because each half's characteristic conjunction is already legible to its own automaton, and the other half's certified types serve merely as an entry ticket.

This is also where the two incidental defects sit, and their co-location is not a coincidence. (D1) is a failure of the cut index to be a *complete* cut datum (the top-level literals do not determine the transducer's initial state). (D2) is a failure to distinguish the cut's *actual* type from a *certified* type in the statement of a linear validity. Both are precisely the bookkeeping that Reynolds' one-sided engine never has to do, because a floored Ockhamist frame has no cut — and both are therefore structurally predictable places for a two-sided transposition to slip. The combinatorial half of the question (which ω-regular conditions are needed, and how much the alphabet must grow) turns out to be entirely determined by the logical half: every alphabet enlargement traces to clause (b)'s need for a state-expressible index, and no enlargement at all traces to clause (a).

## Risks & Mitigations

- **Risk: Theorem 1's step (4) is the single point of failure of the whole argument, and it is a claim about the clause-(b) violation language's *intended* semantics rather than about a formal definition.** Finding 5.1 defines a forward violation as "`γ̄` true at position `i` of the bi-infinite word whose forward half is `v` and whose cut past-type is `pt`", which is a device rather than a word, made precise by Lemma 5.1's transducer. *Mitigation*: step (4) is argued at the level where the object *is* well-defined — the linear semantics of a pure-future linear formula at position `0` quantifies only over positions `≥ 0`, so the value is the same for every bi-infinite extension of `v` and a fortiori for every `pt` — and it is argued without appeal to `sepZ`'s behaviour on an already-pure formula, which was the one route that would have required an unverified claim about what `sep`/`package` return. If Finding 5.1's definition were ever made precise in a way that *did* consult `Π_0` for a pure-future `γ̄`, Theorem 1 would need re-checking; Recommendation 3's (D1) repair is the natural occasion to fix the formulation as well.
- **Risk: Lemma 2's closure bookkeeping (`FutLits ⊆ Sub(φ⁺)`, `δ_{ft} ∈ Sub(φ⁺)`, `⟐δ_{ft} ∈ fcl(φ⁺)`) is a chain of four containments through `ecl(φ)` and the `a_i`, and the source report states the first of them (`PastLits ⊆ cl₀(φ) ⊆ fcl(φ⁺)`) only in passing inside Lemma 4.1's proof.** *Mitigation*: the chain is written out step by step in Finding 2(1) with the clause of the source report licensing each link, and it is the same chain Lemma 4.1 already relies on, so a failure would break Lemma 4.1 and the full-product lemma before it broke Lemma 2. The one link this report adds is `⟐δ_{ft} ∈ Sub(φ⁺)` via the `a_i` inside `θ_f`, which is Lemma 2.6's own content.
- **Risk: Step 3′ is one link longer than Step 3 and its chain order matters** — the seed must be of the opposite polarity to the first certification extracted. *Mitigation*: the chain is written out with each link's input named (Finding 3, items 2-5), and the alternative order (seed a `pt_0`, extract `ft_lin` first) is symmetric under `reflectTime`, so there is no hidden direction-dependence. The chain is also strictly shorter than it looks: items 4 and 5 are the two halves of badness-freedom that the source report's Step 3 also needs.
- **Risk: the elimination strictly shrinks both recognized languages, and a future use of the automata not yet in the architecture might consume the larger language.** *Mitigation*: every current consumer was enumerated (Finding 5's table: Findings 5.2, 5.3, 5.4, 7.2, 14.4, Decision 5) and the asymmetry between antecedent-consuming and hypothesis-consuming sites is stated as a general fact (Finding 8), so a new consumer can be classified on sight. A new consumer of the *hypothesis* kind would need its own seed, and Lemma 2 supplies one at every chronicle node, not just the anchor.
- **Risk: (D2) is a defect in a load-bearing claim of the source report (Claim 1⁺ feeds Claim 2⁺ feeds Lemma 7⁺, the consistency of `φ⁺`), and this report asserts it rather than re-deriving Lemma 7⁺ under the repair.** *Mitigation*: the repair is stated precisely and its effect traced to the end (Finding 6 (D2): Claim 2⁺ still yields `⊢ θ_f → ¬χ₁⁺`, then `⊢ θ_f → ⊡¬χ₁⁺` by `⊡`-stability, so Lemma 7⁺ is unaffected), and the conditional form being cited is a theorem the source report already proves at its Finding 7.3 with the pasting axioms. What is *not* done here is a line-by-line re-derivation of Lemma 7⁺ under the repaired statement; that is in scope for whoever applies Recommendation 4, not for this dispatch, whose question is (Q3).
- **Risk: Finding 4(ii) is at the *recalled* tier and a reader may take it as held.** *Mitigation*: it is marked at the point of use, the corpus sweep that establishes the absence is reported with the commands run, and the verdict is explicitly rested on Finding 4(i) alone, which is derived from the rule inventory.
- **Risk: this report is read as advancing the completeness programme.** It does not. It removes a redundancy from a paper proof whose final-frame step rests on two unlanded pieces and whose automata rest on an unproved determinization theorem. *Mitigation*: stated in the status vocabulary, in Finding 7, and here.
