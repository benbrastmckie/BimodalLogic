## Key Findings

### 1. BX11 Forces Linearity of Witnesses — The Tree IS a Chain

The BX11 axiom (`temp_linearity`) states:
```
F(φ) ∧ F(ψ) → F(φ ∧ ψ) ∨ F(φ ∧ F(ψ)) ∨ F(F(φ) ∧ ψ)
```

This says: any two future witnesses are **linearly ordered** — either they coincide, or one comes strictly before the other. The "tree" generated by multiple F-obligations is therefore **not a branching tree** but a **path** (linear chain). This is the critical structural fact:

- If M has F-obligations {F(ψ₁), ..., F(ψₙ)}, BX11 inductively shows all witnesses can be arranged in a single linear sequence.
- The "tree" degenerates to a chain: ψ_{π(1)} is witnessed first, then ψ_{π(2)} at a later point, etc., for some permutation π.
- This means the "tree-to-linear embedding" step is trivial — the tree is already linear.

**BX11 consequence**: For any two F-obligations F(ψᵢ), F(ψⱼ) in an MCS M, exactly one of the following holds at some future point:
- Both ψᵢ and ψⱼ (simultaneous witness)
- ψᵢ first, then F(ψⱼ) (ψᵢ's witness sees F(ψⱼ))
- ψⱼ first, then F(ψᵢ) (ψⱼ's witness sees F(ψᵢ))

### 2. The Core Challenge: Sequentially Resolving Without Killing

The strategy becomes: resolve F-obligations **one at a time in sequence**, preserving remaining obligations via the F-carry mechanism. The current `fwd_chain` in `CanonicalModel.lean` already uses a schedule, and the `fwd_succ_f_carry` theorem already proves:

```
-- F-formulas persist through non-resolving steps
theorem fwd_succ_f_carry (M : Set Formula) (h_mcs : SetMaximalConsistent M) (ψ : Formula)
    (h_not_F : Formula.some_future ψ ∉ M) :
    f_carry M ⊆ fwd_succ M h_mcs ψ
```

This proves persistence of F-formulas when NOT resolving. But when we resolve F(ψ), the other F-formulas are **not** guaranteed to persist — this is the exact blocker documented in the prior research. The counterexample:
> M contains F(p), F(q), G(p → G(¬q)). Resolving p: the seed {p} ∪ g_content(M) forces G(¬q), which kills F(q).

### 3. What BX11 Actually Buys Us — The Sequential Resolution Schedule

BX11 provides a way to **order** the F-obligations, but it does NOT provide a way to extend a resolving seed while preserving ALL remaining obligations. What it does provide:

**After** resolving F(ψ₁) at step n₁, the resulting MCS M₁ still contains F(ψ₂) (by BX11's disjunctive structure applied within M₁). Specifically, from BX11 in M:
```
F(ψ₁) ∧ F(ψ₂) → F(ψ₁ ∧ ψ₂) ∨ F(ψ₁ ∧ F(ψ₂)) ∨ F(F(ψ₁) ∧ ψ₂)
```
The resolving step for ψ₁ produces an MCS M₁ containing ψ₁. What does M₁ contain about ψ₂?

The g_content of M flows to M₁ (via `fwd_succ_g_content`). But G-formulas from M only give G-formulas at M₁, not F-formulas. The critical question is whether F(ψ₂) ∈ M₁.

**Key insight**: In M₁, we have ψ₁ and g_content(M). If BX11 gave us F(ψ₁ ∧ F(ψ₂)) in M, then in the witness world we'd have ψ₁ ∧ F(ψ₂), giving F(ψ₂) in M₁. But BX11 is a disjunction — we don't know *which* disjunct holds. This non-determinism is the fundamental difficulty.

### 4. The Boneyard Already Has the Solution — DRM Chains + Bounded Witness

Investigation of `Theories/Bimodal/Boneyard/ChainCompleteness/Bundle/ResolvingChain.lean` reveals the existing solution to this exact problem:

**DeferralRestrictedMCS (DRM)**: A restricted MCS within `deferralClosure(root)` (a finite set). The successor uses a `simplified_restricted_seed`:
```
g_content(u) ∪ {ψ ∨ F(ψ) | F(ψ) ∈ u, ψ ∈ deferralClosure} ∪ {p_step_blocking}
```

The **deferral disjunctions** `{ψ ∨ F(ψ) | F(ψ) ∈ u}` are the key. Each step either resolves F(ψ) (produces ψ) or defers it (produces F(ψ) again). This NEVER kills an F-obligation — it merely defers it.

The **bounded witness theorem** (`CanonicalTaskRelation.lean`) then provides forward_F:
- If F(ψ) ∈ M at step t, the obligation is either resolved (ψ ∈ M_{t+1}) or deferred (F(ψ) ∈ M_{t+1})
- F-nesting depth is bounded by `closure_F_bound(root)` = max_F_depth + 2
- Since the closure is finite and nesting eventually runs out, ψ MUST be resolved within bounded steps
- Formally: if F^n(ψ) ∈ M_t and F^{n+1}(ψ) ∉ M_t, then there's a CanonicalTask_forward chain delivering ψ in exactly n steps

The relevant theorem chain:
- `iter_F_not_mem_closureWithNeg`: iter_F n ψ ∉ closureWithNeg for n ≥ closure_F_bound
- `bounded_witness`: If F^n(ψ) ∈ u and F^{n+1}(ψ) ∉ u and chain of length n from u to v, then ψ ∈ v
- This gives forward_F for all ψ ∈ deferralClosure(root)

### 5. Why the Finite Tree Is the DRM Chain

Conceptually, the DRM chain IS the "finite tree embedded into Int":
- The "tree" of witnesses has nodes for each F-obligation resolution at each depth
- Since F-nesting depth is bounded by `closure_F_bound(root)`, the tree depth is bounded
- BX11 linearizes the witnesses (the tree is a chain)
- The DRM chain with deferral disjunctions implements exactly this: deferred = go deeper in tree, resolved = leaf

The embedding into Int is trivial: just use the DRM chain indices (Nat for forward, negative Int for backward).

### 6. Why the Current `bx_fmcs_forward_F` Sorry Cannot Be Closed with Simple Seed Changes

The current code in `CanonicalModel.lean` uses `forward_temporal_witness_seed` (a full MCS over all formulas), whereas the DRM approach requires:
1. A **restricted** MCS over `deferralClosure(root)` only
2. Deferral disjunctions in the seed (not just resolution + g_content)
3. The **bounded witness** argument to collapse finitely many steps into a single forward_F witness

The sorry at line 495 cannot be closed with the existing infrastructure because `bx_fmcs_forward_F` would need:
- Access to `root` (the formula being proved) — but `bx_fmcs` takes no `root` parameter
- A DRM chain construction parameterized by `root`
- The bounded_witness theorem for the specific `root`

This is exactly why restricted temporal coherence (`BFMCS.restricted_temporally_coherent root`) is the correct fix — it parameterizes the property by `root`.

## Recommended Approach

The tree+embedding strategy, properly understood, IS the DRM-chain approach from the boneyard. The recommended implementation:

### Step 1: Parameterize by Root Formula

Replace `bx_fmcs_forward_F` with a `root`-parameterized version:
```lean
theorem bx_fmcs_restricted_forward_F (root : Formula)
    (M₀ : Set Formula) (h₀ : SetMaximalConsistent M₀)
    (t : Int) (ψ : Formula) (h_dc : ψ ∈ deferralClosure root)
    (h_F : Formula.some_future ψ ∈ (bx_fmcs M₀ h₀).mcs t) :
    ∃ s : Int, t < s ∧ ψ ∈ (bx_fmcs M₀ h₀).mcs s
```

### Step 2: Build a DRM-Based Successor

The key is to replace `fwd_succ` with a deferral-based successor:
```lean
/-- Seed: g_content(M) ∪ {ψ ∨ F(ψ) | F(ψ) ∈ M, ψ ∈ deferralClosure root} -/
def deferred_fwd_seed (root : Formula) (M : Set Formula) : Set Formula :=
  g_content M ∪ {φ | ∃ ψ ∈ deferralClosure root,
    Formula.some_future ψ ∈ M ∧ φ = deferralDisjunction ψ}
```

This seed is always consistent (it's a subset of M plus theorems) and produces DRM-compatible successors.

### Step 3: Apply the Bounded Witness Argument

For F(ψ) ∈ M_t with ψ ∈ deferralClosure(root):
- F-nesting depth of F(ψ) is bounded by `closure_F_bound(root)`
- The deferral chain eventually resolves ψ within `closure_F_bound(root)` steps
- Therefore ψ ∈ M_{t+k} for some k ≤ closure_F_bound(root)
- Since k > 0, we get the required strict inequality

### Step 4: Lift to Restricted Temporal Coherence

Prove:
```lean
theorem bx_bfmcs_restricted_temporally_coherent (root : Formula)
    (M₀ : Set Formula) (h₀ : SetMaximalConsistent M₀) :
    (bx_bfmcs M₀ h₀).restricted_temporally_coherent root
```

## Evidence/Examples

### Concrete Proof Sketch for bounded_witness

Suppose F(ψ) ∈ M_t with ψ ∈ deferralClosure(root). Let B = closure_F_bound(root).

**Claim**: ∃ k ≤ B, ψ ∈ M_{t+k}.

**Proof by strong induction on depth n = f_nesting_depth(F(ψ)) within deferralClosure(root)**:

Base case (n = 1): F(ψ) ∈ M_t, F(F(ψ)) ∉ deferralClosure (too deep). The deferral disjunction ψ ∨ F(ψ) is in M_{t+1}'s seed. In M_{t+1} (a DRM), either ψ ∈ M_{t+1} (done) or F(ψ) ∈ M_{t+1}. But F(F(ψ)) ∉ deferralClosure, so the next step's seed won't include F(ψ) ∨ F(F(ψ)) — this forces ψ ∈ M_{t+2} by DRM maximality applied to ψ. So k ≤ 2.

Inductive case (n > 1): The deferral disjunction in M_{t+1} either resolves ψ (k=1) or defers to F(ψ) ∈ M_{t+1}. By IH on M_{t+1} with depth n-1, ψ is resolved within B-(n-1) more steps.

### BX11 Linearization Example

M = {F(p), F(q)}. By BX11: F(p ∧ q) ∨ F(p ∧ F(q)) ∨ F(F(p) ∧ q) holds in M.
- Disjunct 1: Single witness at s resolves both
- Disjunct 2: At s₁ we have p ∧ F(q), then at s₂ > s₁ we have q
- Disjunct 3: At s₁ we have F(p) ∧ q, then at s₂ > s₁ we have p

In all cases the witnesses are linearly ordered: {s} or {s₁, s₂} with s₁ < s₂. The "tree" has depth at most 2 for 2 F-obligations.

**With deferral disjunctions**: M₁ extends M with {p ∨ F(p), q ∨ F(q)}. In M₁:
- If p ∈ M₁ and q ∈ M₁: both resolved at step 1 (BX11 Disjunct 1 case)
- If p ∈ M₁ and F(q) ∈ M₁: need another step for q (BX11 Disjunct 2 case)
- If F(p) ∈ M₁ and q ∈ M₁: need another step for p (BX11 Disjunct 3 case)
- If F(p) ∈ M₁ and F(q) ∈ M₁: both deferred (but then next step MUST resolve as depth-1 F-formulas)

### Key Architectural Decision

**The current `bx_fmcs` takes no `root` parameter**. The DRM approach requires knowing `root` to bound F-nesting. Two options:

**Option A**: Add `root` parameter to `bx_fmcs`, making it `bx_fmcs root M₀ h₀` and prove restricted coherence directly. This requires threading `root` through the chain construction.

**Option B**: Build a SEPARATE `bx_fmcs_drm root M₀ h₀` that uses DRM seeds, and prove it satisfies restricted temporal coherence for `root`. The final countermodel uses this DRM-based FMCS. This avoids touching existing proofs.

**Option B is preferred**: It's less disruptive and the existing `bx_fmcs` can remain for the modal stability proofs (box_stable_in_int_chain etc.) which don't need forward_F.

### Compatibility with Existing Proofs

The existing proofs of `box_stable_in_shifted_fmcs`, `modal_forward`, `modal_backward` for `bx_bfmcs` do NOT require `forward_F` — they only use `g_content`/`h_content` propagation and box stability. A parallel DRM-based BFMCS can reuse these proofs while adding restricted temporal coherence.

## Confidence Level

**High (85%)** that the DRM chain approach via deferral disjunctions solves `bx_fmcs_forward_F` for the restricted case.

**Justification**:
- The approach is entirely logic-sound: deferral disjunctions guarantee F-obligations are either resolved or deferred, never killed; bounded nesting depth forces eventual resolution
- The boneyard `ResolvingChain.lean` already implements most of this (though with sorries inside — the key theorems are structurally correct)
- BX11 is not actually needed to prove the bounded resolution — the deferral disjunction mechanism works without needing BX11; BX11 is however needed to prove the overall semantics are correct (linear order)
- `CanonicalTaskRelation.lean` has the `iter_F` and `closure_F_bound` infrastructure

**The key risk (15% failure probability)**:
- The bounded_witness argument requires that when F^{n+1}(ψ) ∉ deferralClosure, the next DRM step doesn't include F(ψ) in its seed (so ψ MUST be chosen). Verifying this requires careful analysis of how DRM maximality interacts with the boundary case. This may require additional lemmas about `deferralClosure` membership at the depth boundary.

**BX11 relevance**: BX11 is NOT the proof engine for forward_F — it's the *semantic* justification for why the Int-indexed linear model is correct (linear witnesses). The actual Lean proof of forward_F goes through the bounded_witness argument, not through BX11.

## Interaction of F and P Obligations

For formulas like F(P(φ)):
- Resolving F(P(φ)) at step t produces P(φ) at step t+1
- P(φ) at t+1 requires a past witness at some step s < t+1, but s could be t (within the chain)
- The backward chain handles P-obligations symmetrically via `bwd_pred` and `p_carry`
- The depth bound works identically: `iter_P_not_mem_closureWithNeg` (analogously defined) gives bounded resolution for P-formulas

Nesting interaction (F inside P inside F etc.) is bounded by the formula's structural depth, which is already captured by `closure_F_bound` (which uses `max_F_depth_in_closure` — a measure on the closure, not just on the root formula).
