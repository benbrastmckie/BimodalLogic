**Design: separate views from logical rewriting**
- `lrewrite` currently does two different things under one name. With a formula equality it casts, and `lsimp`'s view preparation and the occurrence views in `Positions.lean` fold and unfold defined operators; none of this uses an axiom, a context or a frame class. With an implication or equivalence it builds a derivation through the congruence kernel, needs evidence that survives the scope, and respects polarity. The plan itself says these need separate handlers (§3.1). Please separate them at the surface as well; this is a condition of merging. The formula-equality path leaves `lrewrite` and `lsimp`, which keep logical steps only, and is exposed as the views pair `lunfold` / `lfold`. These are the Views tier and the Derivation tier of the architecture record, and principle 1 there is that no tactic name serves two tiers.
- The views tier is M16, open as `proof_mode_views_tier`. It has one pair of tactics, `lunfold` and `lfold`, each taking a view name, over one table of views: a row gives the name, the folded shape, the unfolded shape and the equality that relates them, `rfl` for a defined operator and a proved formula equality otherwise. A formula equality is used as a view by registering it as a row, and the pair takes no free proof term. One tactic per view was the alternative; it is rejected on count (`Formula` has 26 defined operators and `PlusFormula` 22) and because each language extension would add tactic syntax. What this PR contributes is the pair and the first rows of that table, for what its formula-equality path handles today. The full tier stays under `proof_mode_views_tier`: a row for every defined operator, the elaborators' operator matching on the same table, and the placement at layer 0 or 1. M17 does not wait on M16; M16 follows M0 and takes over the rows contributed here.
- Views sit below the derivation tactics and import none of the rewriting machinery, so that a semantic mode can use them too.

**Design: file rules by operator family**
- The architecture record files each rule by the operators its statement names. There are three families: Boolean, Modal and Temporal. Modal holds `□` and `◇` and, in TM⁺, `⊡` and its dual `⟐`; Temporal holds future and past together. An interaction is its own module named by exactly the families it joins, Boolean included (`ModalBoolean`, `TemporalBoolean`, `ModalTemporal`, `ModalTemporalBoolean`), and frame class is a suffix (`TemporalZTime`). A pure family module has no implication in any statement. The point is principle 6: a later language extension adds a family module and its interaction modules and changes nothing that exists. The same names are used for the rule modules, the tactic modules, the test files, the guide pages, the interface classes and the library fragments ("One scheme, used six times").
- `Congruence.lean` already has the right unit, one `Step` per operator position; the record's "Logical rewriting" paragraph says a step belongs to its operator's family. Please split the steps by family (the modal step with the modal rules, the Until and Since steps with the temporal rules) and keep the generic `Kit`, `Step.comp` and path composition on their own.
- `⊡` is filed under Modal, with the S5 rules stated once over a necessity operator given as a parameter and instantiated at `□` and at `⊡`. The shared statement is a structure taking the operator as a field, over your `Kit`, instantiated once per necessity operator per language: for TM at `□`, for TM⁺ at `□` and at `⊡`. Its fields are theorems and not axioms, because TM `□`, TM⁺ `□` and `⊡` have three different axiom bases, so each instance supplies some fields by derivation. So the `□` step and the `⊡` step should be one step taking that structure, not two definitions. A prototype against your `Kit` and `Step` shows that one definition gives the three steps (TM `□`, TM⁺ `□` and `⊡`). It does not show whether this also removes the ⊡ iteration limit noted above; please check. Within a module the rules for `□` alone come first and those naming `⊡` are a section of their own. Whether `⊡` should be a family of its own is under examination (`investigate_stability_as_modal_family`, M23; the question "Is `⊡` rightly in the Modal family?" under "Open decisions" of `records/theorem-library.md`); the design starts from Modal and nothing waits on that report.
- `Kit` and `Step` are generic over the language, but every step is then defined twice (`tmBox` / `plusBox`, 13 TM and 14 TM⁺ definitions) and the elaborators choose between them by name pairs. This is the start of the object-level interface of M8 (`proof_mode_abstract_frontend_interface`): one class per family carrying its rules, with TM and TM⁺ as instances. Please make TM and TM⁺ two instances of one class carrying the steps, or at least add no further name pairs, so that M8 does not have to undo this. The Modal family's shared S5 statement is not one of those classes: it is the structure above, with the operator as a field, and a language supplies it at each necessity operator it has. A class indexed by the operator has merits: call sites need not name the instance, and two instances on one formula type do coexist. It is rejected because instance resolution is keyed on how the operator is written and fails on an operator presented through a definition, and because the elaborators have the operator in hand anyway. `Kit` and `Step` are structures already, so this continues the choice you made.
- The seed rules for `lsimp` (`boxConjIff`, `diamondDisjIff`, `s5DiamondBox`) are Modal-with-Boolean rules and belong in `ModalBoolean`, not in a simplification module. `ScopeConversions.lean` likewise: `□` to `G`/`H` is `ModalTemporal`, and `□` to `⊡` is within the Modal family, in its `⊡` section.
