### 2. What the Key Papers Actually Say About Representation for Tense Logics

**Venema 1991 (Dissertation, Ch. 2 + Appendix A)**: Gives the full framework for BAO representation in a multi-modal setting. The appendix defines:
- Complex algebras (Cm𝔉) from frames (Definition A17)
- Canonical structures (Cs𝔄) from ultrafilters (Definition A22)  
- Embedding algebras (Em𝔄 = Cm Cs 𝔄) (Definition A22)
- The key fact: At 𝔉⁺ ≅ 𝔉 (Theorem A21) — atom structures of complex algebras recover the frame

**Theorem 2.2.5 in Venema 1991** proves: for Sahlqvist equations η, 𝔄 ⊨ η iff Em 𝔄 ⊨ η. This is the canonicity result that makes Jónsson-Tarski representation work for Sahlqvist varieties.

**Critical point for tense logics**: Venema 1991 §2.3 proves Theorem 2.3.3: Sahlqvist *tense* formulas persist from discrete general frames to their underlying Kripke frames. The proof relies on *rudimentary valuations* (Definition 2.3.9), which use tense paths — requiring that every diamond has a converse. This is exactly the situation in TM logic (G/H are converses; S/U are temporal converses).

**Venema 1997 (Atom Structures)**: Theorem 1.4 establishes that for **conjugated** varieties of BAOs (where operators come in conjugate tuples), all five nice properties collapse:
- AO = AD = AC (atom-corresponding = atom-determined = atom-canonical)
- All conjugated varieties are atom-complex and atom-elementary

This is directly relevant because tense operators (G paired with H, and the implicit F/P from U/S) are conjugated. Since/Until operators as binary operators also have conjugate structure since they have natural converse operations.

**GHV 2003**: Shows limitations — there exist 2^ℵ₀ canonical BAO varieties not generated by any elementary class of frames. However, this negative result involves **non-conjugated** operators. For conjugated tense algebras, the situation is much better (per Venema 1997).

**de Rijke-Venema 1995**: Extends the Sahlqvist theorem to arbitrary BAO similarity types. The key result (Theorem 3.5): Sahlqvist varieties are canonical. Applies to S/U algebras since their axioms are Sahlqvist equations. The paper also defines:
- Complex algebras 𝔉⁺ and canonical structures 𝔄₊ for arbitrary types
- The canonical embedding algebra Em 𝔄 = (Cs 𝔄)⁺
- Theorem 3.8: For canonical varieties, equivalence of Sahlqvist equations can be checked on atom structures

**Venema 1993 (Anti-Axioms)**: Shows that **orthodox** axiom systems (using only MP, TG, SUB — no IRR rule) yield varieties closed under ultraproducts. This matters because the BX system for TM logic uses the IRR rule for irreflexivity, which complicates the algebraic picture. However, the S/U temporal part itself is orthodoxly axiomatizable (Burgess 1982, Venema 1993 Since/Until).

### 3. The S5 Component Complicates the Picture

The TM logic combines S5 modality (Box/Diamond) with temporal S/U operators. For the algebraic treatment:

- **S5 algebras** are well-understood: monadic algebras where Box is an interior operator with the S5 identity (¬□a → □¬□a). The existing `TenseS5Algebra.lean` already captures this.
- **Tense S/U algebras** are BAOs with binary operators satisfying the Burgess axioms.
- **The combination** requires interaction axioms (MF, TF, TA, TL) that ensure modal formulas are temporally persistent.

The Jónsson-Tarski representation for the combined algebra requires showing that the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra embeds into the complex algebra of the canonical frame class. The key question is: **which frame class?** For TM, this should be the class of task frames (S5 equivalence classes on a strict linear temporal order).

### 5. Alternative Mathematical Approaches

**Approach A: Stone Duality (Category-Theoretic)**

Instead of explicit ultrafilter constructions, use the Stone duality between BAOs and descriptive general frames:
- The Lindenbaum algebra L is a BAO
- Its Stone space is the canonical frame Cs(L) 
- L embeds into Cm(Cs(L)) by the Stone representation
- The representation theorem becomes: L is isomorphic to a subalgebra of the complex algebra of the canonical task frame class

*Advantage*: More abstract, potentially easier to formalize in Lean 4 (Mathlib has Stone duality)
*Disadvantage*: Less constructive; harder to extract concrete countermodels

**Approach B: Direct Ultrafilter Construction (Current Path)**

The existing `UltrafilterMCS.lean` already builds the ultrafilter world. The representation would:
1. Define the Lindenbaum-Tarski algebra (already in `LindenbaumQuotient.lean`)
2. Show it's an STSA (already in `TenseS5Algebra.lean`)
3. Take ultrafilters as worlds (already in `AlgebraicRepresentation.lean`)
4. Define frame relations on ultrafilters using the canonical relation
5. Prove the algebra embeds into the complex algebra of this frame
6. Show the frame satisfies the task frame axioms

*Advantage*: Closest to existing code; well-understood technique
*Disadvantage*: The binary S/U operators require ternary relations in the canonical structure

**Approach C: Parametric Representation (Existing Innovation)**

The existing `ParametricRepresentation.lean` already takes a novel approach: parametric over the duration type D. This sidesteps the problem of characterizing the canonical timeline.

*For Jónsson-Tarski*: The representation theorem should similarly be parametric — show that for ANY totally ordered abelian group D satisfying the frame constraints, the Lindenbaum algebra embeds into the complex algebra of D-parametric task frames.

**Recommended**: Approach B+C hybrid. Keep the parametric innovation from ParametricRepresentation, but restructure to make the algebraic embedding (Lindenbaum → Cm(canonical frame)) explicit.

### 6. Handling Since/Until Algebraically

The key algebraic insight from the literature:

S and U are **binary** normal operators (additive in each argument). In BAO terms:
- U induces a ternary relation R_U where R_U(t, s, guard-region)  
- The complex algebra operation is: m_U(X, Y) = {t | ∃s>t: s∈X ∧ ∀u(t<u<s → u∈Y)}

The Burgess axioms A1a–A7a and their mirror images are all **Sahlqvist equations** when translated to the algebraic setting (as confirmed by de Rijke-Venema 1995). This means:
1. They define a canonical variety (Sahlqvist theorem)
2. The corresponding class of atom structures is elementary (Venema 1997, Theorem 1.4 for conjugated case)
3. Jónsson-Tarski representation works cleanly

The interaction axioms (MF, TF, TA, TL) combining Box with G/H are also Sahlqvist, so the full STSA variety is Sahlqvist and hence canonical.

## Evidence/Examples

### Citation 1: Venema 1991, Theorem A21
"𝔉 ≅ At Cm 𝔉" — taking atom structures of complex algebras recovers the frame. This is one half of the Jónsson-Tarski duality.

### Citation 2: Venema 1997, Theorem 1.4
For **conjugated** varieties: AO = AD = AC, and all are atom-complex and atom-elementary. Since tense operators are conjugated, the representation for tense algebras has these strong properties.

### Citation 3: de Rijke-Venema 1995, Theorem 3.5  
Sahlqvist varieties are canonical. Since all BX axioms (including S/U axioms A1–A7 and interaction axioms MF/TF/TA/TL) are Sahlqvist, the STSA variety is canonical.

Orthodox axiom systems (no IRR rule) ensure the corresponding variety is closed under ultraproducts. The BX system uses IRR for irreflexivity, but the algebraic representation should work with the orthodox fragment (the Burgess axioms are orthodox).
