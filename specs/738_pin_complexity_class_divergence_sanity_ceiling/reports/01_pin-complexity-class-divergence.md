## Findings

### 1. The two sources, read in full

- **Source A (706 Q4, lines 355-358)** states the consequence from the certificate's side explicitly: if every non-validity had a sliced certificate with `n, nb, nm, nf ≤ 2^{poly(κ)}`, then "since the checker runs in time polynomial in `n · 2^κ · (nb + nm + nf)`, non-validity would be in NEXPTIME and validity in co-NEXPTIME, so 2EXPTIME ⊆ co-NEXPTIME. That is not refuted, but it is disbelieved (it is the exponential analogue of EXPTIME ⊆ co-NP)." Its hardness claim at line 348-350 is phrased for validity: "Hence L⁺ ℤ-time validity is 2EXPTIME-hard (Vardi and Stockmeyer 1985, as reported in `reynolds_2001/sec01`)". Line 49 (Executive Summary) compresses this to "would place a 2EXPTIME-hard problem in co-NEXPTIME": the problem it is placing is validity, the complement of what the certificate class decides.
- **Source B (703 round 2, section 1.6, lines 206-243)** states C1 as "CTL\* satisfiability reduces to L⁺ ℤ-time satisfiability" and draws "ℤ-time validity of L⁺ is 2EXPTIME-hard" as the consequence. Its "what remains unknown" bullet (lines 236-238) reads: "A singly exponential certificate checkable in time polynomial in its size would put a 2EXPTIME-hard problem in NEXPTIME, which is widely disbelieved but not refuted." The certificate in B's own framing (section 1.4, "the finite object that represents a countermodel"; section 1.5, "what the landed certificate actually presents") certifies a countermodel, i.e. non-validity, i.e. satisfiability of the negation. So the "2EXPTIME-hard problem" B places in NEXPTIME is satisfiability — the problem its own reduction C1 targets directly.
- **Neither source names the other's class.** A never says "NEXPTIME ⊇ 2EXPTIME" and B never says "co-NEXPTIME". The appearance of divergence arises entirely from reading the two one-liners (A line 49, B line 238) side by side without the problem each is placing.

### 2. Why the two statements are the same statement

- **Both problems are 2EXPTIME-hard.** C1 reduces CTL\* satisfiability to L⁺ satisfiability, so L⁺ ℤ-time satisfiability is 2EXPTIME-hard directly. Validity is the complement of satisfiability-of-the-negation, and negation is a trivial formula map; since 2EXPTIME is a deterministic class closed under complement, hardness transfers to validity. This is exactly why A can write the hardness claim for validity while B's reduction targets satisfiability — no discrepancy.
- **The containments are equivalent.** `2EXPTIME ⊆ NEXPTIME` iff `2EXPTIME ⊆ co-NEXPTIME`: if `L ∈ 2EXPTIME` then its complement is in 2EXPTIME too, so the complement is in NEXPTIME, so `L ∈ co-NEXPTIME`; and symmetrically. A chose the validity/co-NEXPTIME reading, B the satisfiability/NEXPTIME reading, of one fact.
- **What the "disbelieved" collapse is.** With the standard inclusions `NEXPTIME ⊆ EXPSPACE ⊆ 2EXPTIME`, the containment would force `NEXPTIME = EXPSPACE = 2EXPTIME`. It is not refuted: by the usual padding argument, `NEXPTIME ≠ 2EXPTIME` would imply `NP ≠ EXPTIME`, which is open. A's analogy ("the exponential analogue of EXPTIME ⊆ co-NP") is exact, since `EXPTIME ⊆ co-NP` iff `EXPTIME = NP`, also open. Both sources' "not refuted, but disbelieved" is therefore correct and consistent. (These are textbook closure and inclusion facts, not findings of this report; they are used only to check that the two sources agree.)
- **Scope of the ruling.** It applies to the stated consequence only. The soundness of C1 and the polynomial-time cost of the translation (B notes "the closure grows linearly") are 713's content and remain ARGUED.
