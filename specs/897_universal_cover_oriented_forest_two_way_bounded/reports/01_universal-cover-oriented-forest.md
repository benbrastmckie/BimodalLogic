## Executive Summary

- **A sorry-free route exists and is fully specified below.** Every ingredient the task names
  (`IsOrientedForest` of the cover, double seriality, surjectivity of the endpoint projection,
  `TwoWayBounded`, and the assembly through `saturation_of_orientedForest` and
  `plus_invariance_of_isZTime`) reduces to statements this report gives explicitly, with no step
  that needs a `sorry`, a new axiom, or a weakened hypothesis.
- **Mathlib's `Combinatorics/Quiver/Covering.lean` is not a usable starting point and should be
  recorded as rejected.** It defines only the *property* of being a covering (`Prefunctor.IsCovering`
  as star/costar bijectivity) and contains **no construction of any cover**; the single consumer in
  the whole Mathlib tree is `Quiver/Schreier.lean:142`. There is no reduced-path predicate, no path
  reduction, no universal cover of a quiver or of a `SimpleGraph`, and no `Quiver` instance on
  `Digraph` at this pin. Routing through `Quiver` would require building a reduced-path carrier
  anyway *and* would still owe `(SimpleGraph.fromRel ·).IsAcyclic` separately, since that — not
  `IsCovering` — is what `IsOrientedForest` asks for.
- **The carrier should be a dependently-indexed inductive reduced walk**, indexed by its last move's
  (direction, previous vertex). That index makes reducedness *structural* rather than a side
  predicate, and makes the multigraph convention (opposite-edge pair lifts to two distinct edges,
  loop lifts to a ray) fall out of the constructors instead of being imposed. §Findings gives the
  exact declaration.
- **Base points should be taken over all of `W`, not one per connected component.** The cover
  `Σ (origin : W), ReducedWalk origin` is a `|W|`-fold-redundant disjoint union of the components'
  universal covers, which is still an oriented forest, still doubly serial, and has a projection
  that is *more* obviously surjective. This eliminates every connectivity, component-quotient and
  base-point-choice obligation from the construction, at zero cost to the consumers.
- **Two hard-won simplifications, both from existing compiled lemmas.** (1)
  `FrameOver.taskRel_eq_iter` (`IntNormalForm.lean:224`) already proves every `[F.IsRegular]` frame
  over `intOrder` is generated by its one-step relation, so the `hgen` hypothesis that
  `saturation_of_orientedForest'` carries is **free** for the base frame — the headline theorem can
  quantify over an arbitrary regular ℤ-frame with no side condition. (2) `unique lifts` is **not**
  needed: `plus_invariance_of_isZTime` consumes only `forth`, `step` and surjectivity.
- **One real API gap and one real proof obligation.** The gap: `FrameOver.ofStep`
  (`IntNormalForm.lean:456`) carries `[Finite W]` purely to discharge *Saturation* via
  `saturation_of_finite`, and the cover carrier is infinite — a `Saturation`-parameterised sibling
  constructor must be added. The obligation: `(SimpleGraph.fromRel coverStep).IsAcyclic` has no
  Mathlib helper; §Findings gives a reusable rank/parent criterion and a bridge-based proof that
  discharges it without touching `Walk.IsCycle` at all.

**Strategy**: Construction, then transfer. Build a cover whose combinatorial type is better than the
base's (an oriented forest), show the projection is a morphism of the kind under which L⁺ truth is
invariant, and conclude that validity over the restricted class equals validity over the unrestricted
one.

### Step Map

1. **Form the cover.** Given a doubly serial digraph `E` on `W`, take `W`'s connected components as
   *undirected multigraphs with orientation kept*, take each component's universal cover, and take
   the disjoint union. — [Source] §1.2, proof, sentence 2.
2. **The projection is a surjective two-way bounded morphism with unique lifts.** — [Source] §1.2,
   proof, sentence 3; morphism notion defined in §1.1 (`TwoWay R' R f`: homomorphism + *forth* +
   *back*).
3. **The cover is doubly serial.** — [Source] §1.2, proof, sentence 4.
4. **The cover is an oriented forest.** §1.2's original gloss said "directed walks are reduced
   paths, so directed paths between two nodes are unique"; its **amendment** restates the hypothesis
   as *the underlying undirected multigraph is a forest*, and records that the cover delivers that
   directly ("the universal cover of a connected undirected multigraph is a tree, and a disjoint
   union of trees is a forest"). — [Source] §1.2 amendment, "Derived: the cover already delivers the
   strengthened hypothesis".
5. **The multigraph convention is load-bearing.** An opposite-edge pair `u → v`, `v → u` lifts to
   **two distinct** cover edges to two distinct nodes; a loop `u → u` lifts to a ray; hence the cover
   is a *simple* tree with no directed 2-cycles. — [Source] §1.2 amendment, "Multigraph convention,
   carried forward"; restated as a precision note at 878 finding 1.
6. **Saturation of the cover follows** from steps 3-4 by the oriented-forest lemma (steps 1-5 of
   §1.2's own numbered argument). — [Source] §1.2, numbered steps 1-5. **Already landed** as
   `saturation_of_orientedForest`; not re-derived here.
7. **Transfer.** With the cover regular and the base regular, the invariance theorem applies and the
   cover refutes whatever the base refuted. — [Source] 878 finding 1, "Step A (the cover)".

### Dependencies

- Steps 3, 4, 5 depend on step 1 (and step 5 is a property of step 1's *convention*, not a

- Step 6 depends on steps 3 and 4.
- Step 7 depends on steps 2, 6, and regularity of the base.

**F2 — `FrameOver.ofStep` cannot build the cover frame: it carries `[Finite W]`.**
`FormalSystem/Semantics/IntNormalForm.lean:456`:

```lean
def ofStep {W : Type} [Finite W] [Nonempty W] (R₁ : W → W → Prop)
    (fwd : ∀ w, ∃ u, R₁ w u) (bwd : ∀ w, ∃ v, R₁ v w) : FrameOver intOrder
```

Reading its body (lines 456-488), the `[Finite W]` binder is consumed at **exactly one place**: the
final argument `TaskFrame.saturation_of_finite (ofStepRel R₁)`, whose own signature is
`{W : Type} [Finite W] (R : W → D → W → Prop) : Saturation R` (`TaskFrame.lean:1868`). Every other
field is finiteness-free — `comp` from `iter_add`, reflection from `ofStepRel`'s sign-guarded shape,
`serial` from `fwd`/`bwd` via `exists_iter_fwd`/`exists_iter_bwd` (`IntNormalForm.lean:412`/`422`),
`limit` from `TaskFrame.limit_of_succOrder` (`TaskFrame.lean:1608`, which binds `[SuccOrder D]`
`[NoMaxOrder D]` and no finiteness).

The universal cover's carrier is infinite whenever the base has any edge, so the cover frame cannot
be presented by `ofStep`. The fix is a one-for-one generalization in the same module:

```lean
def ofStepSat {W : Type} [Nonempty W] (R₁ : W → W → Prop)
    (fwd : ∀ w, ∃ u, R₁ w u) (bwd : ∀ w, ∃ v, R₁ v w)
    (hsat : TaskFrame.Saturation (ofStepRel R₁)) : FrameOver intOrder
```

with `ofStep R₁ fwd bwd := ofStepSat R₁ fwd bwd (TaskFrame.saturation_of_finite _)` and the existing
`ofStep_isRegular` / `ofStep_taskRel` / `ofStep_step` restated at `ofStepSat` (the existing three then
follow by citation). This is a non-breaking refactor: `ofStep`'s signature, type, and every
downstream consumer — `IntPresentation.lean:142`, `Nomic/ZTime.lean:121`, `IntNormalForm.lean:531` —
are untouched.

**F3 — `hgen` is free for the base frame.** `FrameOver.taskRel_eq_iter`
(`IntNormalForm.lean:224`):

```lean
theorem taskRel_eq_iter (F : FrameOver intOrder) [F.IsRegular] (w u : F.WorldState) (d : ℤ) :
    F.TaskRel w d u ↔
      (0 ≤ d → iter F.step d.natAbs w u) ∧ (d ≤ 0 → iter F.step d.natAbs u w)
```

The right-hand side is `ofStepRel F.step w d u` unfolded (`ofStepRel`, `IntNormalForm.lean:391`). So
**every** regular frame over `intOrder` is generated by its one-step relation
`F.step := fun w u => F.TaskRel w 1 u` (`IntNormalForm.lean:182`), with no extra hypothesis. Two
consequences:

- The headline theorem of this task can be stated over an arbitrary `F : FrameOver intOrder` with
  `[F.IsRegular]`, taking `E := F.step`, with no generation side condition and no carrier
  identification between `F.WorldState` and some ambient `W`.
- Double seriality of `E := F.step` is free too: `TaskFrame.Serial` (`TaskFrame.lean:776`) reads
  `∀ w x, 0 ≤ x → (∃ u, R w x u) ∧ (∃ v, R v x w)`, and `F.serial w 1 zero_le_one` is exactly
  `fwd`/`bwd` for `F.step`. **The base's "doubly serial" hypothesis is therefore not a hypothesis at
  all** — it is a projection of `[F.IsRegular]`.

### Recommendations

Five design decisions and three new-content items. All are sorry-free by construction; none defers
anything.

#### Decision 1 — Reject the Quiver route; build a bespoke carrier.

Recorded with reasons at **F5**. The module remains a single new file,
`FormalSystem/Semantics/UniversalCover.lean`, importing `FormalSystem.Semantics.OrientedForest`
(which transitively brings `IntNormalForm` and `Mathlib.Combinatorics.SimpleGraph.Acyclic`) and
`FormalSystem.Semantics.HistoryMorphism`.

#### Decision 2 — Base points over all of `W`, not one per component.

Carrier: `Σ (origin : W), ReducedWalk E origin`. This is the disjoint union, over every `w : W`, of
the universal cover of `w`'s component pointed at `w` — i.e. each component's cover appears once per
vertex of that component. Every property the consumers need is preserved by that redundancy:

- a disjoint union of forests is a forest, so `acyclic` is unaffected;
- double seriality is per-point;
- the endpoint projection is surjective *more* easily (the length-zero walk at `w` maps to `w`);
- `TwoWayBounded.forth`/`step` are per-point.

What it buys: **no** `Quiver.WeaklyConnectedComponent`, no `SimpleGraph.ConnectedComponent`, no
base-point choice function, no `Classical.choice` introduced by the construction, and no
connectivity lemma. This is a presentational deviation from the source's "disjoint union of the
universal covers of its components" and is flagged as such in §Literature Proof Structure.

#### Decision 3 — Reducedness as a structural index, not a side predicate.

The pivotal design point. A step of a walk in the underlying undirected multigraph is a
**direction + target**: from the current vertex `a`, either go *forward* to `b` using `E a b`, or go
*backward* to `b` using `E b a`. Because `E` is `Prop`-valued, the base edge traversed is determined
by the ordered pair, so the multigraph never has to be built.

Writing a move as `(d, v)` with `d : Bool` the direction and `v` the target, and letting `c` be the
vertex *before* the previous move, the no-immediate-backtracking condition on consecutive moves
`(d₁, v₁)`, `(d₂, v₂)` works out to exactly

> backtrack ⟺ `d₂ = ¬d₁` **and** `v₂ = c`.

Carrying `(d₁, c)` as an **index** of the inductive makes that condition a constructor side
condition on data already in the type:

```lean
/-- `Cover E o e m`: a reduced walk in the undirected multigraph underlying `E`, from `o` to `e`,
whose last move had direction and pre-vertex `m` (`none` for the length-zero walk). -/
inductive Cover (E : W → W → Prop) (o : W) : W → Option (Bool × W) → Type
  | nil : Cover E o o none
  | fwd {a b : W} {m} (p : Cover E o a m) (h : E a b) (hred : m ≠ some (false, b)) :
      Cover E o b (some (true, a))
  | bwd {a b : W} {m} (p : Cover E o a m) (h : E b a) (hred : m ≠ some (true, b)) :
      Cover E o b (some (false, a))

/-- A point of the cover: a reduced walk bundled with its three indices. -/
structure CoverPt (E : W → W → Prop) : Type where
  origin : W
  endpt : W
  last : Option (Bool × W)
  walk : Cover E origin endpt last
```

**The multigraph convention is forced, not asserted.** Three checks, each a named consumer in the
source (§Literature Proof Structure step 5):

- *An opposite-edge pair lifts to two distinct edges.* With `E a b` and `E b a` both present, from a
  walk ending at `b` whose last move was `fwd` from `a`, the move `fwd` to `a` (using `E b a`) has
  `d₂ = d₁ = true`, so `hred` is satisfied and the move is admitted — it traverses the *other* base
  edge. Only the same-edge reversal (`bwd` back to `a`, i.e. `m = some (true, a)`) is barred.
- *A loop lifts to a ray.* With `E a a`, repeated `fwd` moves have `d₂ = d₁` and are all admitted,
  giving an infinite forward ray; repeated `bwd` moves give the backward ray. The immediate
  `fwd`-then-`bwd` reversal is barred.
- *Hence the cover is a simple tree with no directed 2-cycles.* This is `oppFree` below, and it is
  proved from the direction tag in `last`.

#### Decision 4 — The cover's one-step relation as an inductive, to avoid `HEq`.

Because `fwd`/`bwd` change the `last` index, stating "`q` is `p` extended by one move" as an equation
between `CoverPt`s drags in heterogeneous equality. Stating it as an inductive does not:

```lean
/-- The cover's directed one-step relation. The cover edge keeps the base edge's orientation:
a `fwd` extension points away from its parent, a `bwd` extension points at it. -/
inductive CoverStep (E : W → W → Prop) : CoverPt E → CoverPt E → Prop
  | up {o a b m} (p : Cover E o a m) (h : E a b) (hred : m ≠ some (false, b)) :
      CoverStep E ⟨o, a, m, p⟩ ⟨o, b, some (true, a), p.fwd h hred⟩
  | down {o a b m} (p : Cover E o a m) (h : E b a) (hred : m ≠ some (true, b)) :
      CoverStep E ⟨o, b, some (false, a), p.bwd h hred⟩ ⟨o, a, m, p⟩
```

`cases` on `CoverStep` then yields exactly the two shapes with no index wrangling.

#### Decision 5 — One structural lemma discharges three obligations.

With `CoverPt.len` the walk length (`Cover.length` by recursion) and `CoverPt.par` the drop-last map
(`nil ↦ itself`, `fwd p _ _ ↦ ⟨o, a, m, p⟩`, `bwd p _ _ ↦ ⟨o, a, m, p⟩`), prove **one** lemma by
`cases` on `CoverStep`:

```lean
theorem coverStep_cases {x y : CoverPt E} (h : CoverStep E x y) :
    (y.len = x.len + 1 ∧ y.last = some (true, x.endpt) ∧ y.par = x) ∨
    (x.len = y.len + 1 ∧ x.last = some (false, y.endpt) ∧ x.par = y)
```

It discharges all three `IsOrientedForest` fields' prerequisites:

- `loopFree`: `x = y` makes both disjuncts say `n = n + 1`.
- `oppFree`: from `CoverStep x y` and `CoverStep y x`, the two same-side pairings contradict on
  length, and the two cross pairings agree on length but contradict on the direction tag
  (`some (true, ·)` vs `some (false, ·)`). **This is where "no directed 2-cycles" is proved, and it
  is proved from the tag — the formal counterpart of the multigraph convention.**
- `acyclic`: it is exactly the `hadj` hypothesis of the criterion in **F7**, read through
  `fromRel_adj`.

#### New content item A — a reusable acyclicity criterion (**F7**).

```lean
theorem SimpleGraph.isAcyclic_of_rank_parent {V : Type*} {G : SimpleGraph V}
    (rk : V → ℕ) (par : V → V) (hle : ∀ v, rk (par v) ≤ rk v)
    (hadj : ∀ ⦃u v⦄, G.Adj u v →
      (par v = u ∧ rk u < rk v) ∨ (par u = v ∧ rk v < rk u)) :
    G.IsAcyclic
```

*Proof, with no `Walk.IsCycle` reasoning.* Use `isAcyclic_iff_forall_adj_isBridge`
(`Acyclic.lean:182`) and `isBridge_iff` (`Connected.lean:763`, which is `.rfl`). Given `G.Adj u v`,
without loss of generality `par v = u` and `rk u < rk v`; it suffices to show
`¬ (G.deleteEdges {s(u, v)}).Reachable u v`. Put `Anc x := ∃ k, par^[k] x = v`.

1. `Anc v` holds (`k = 0`).
2. `Anc` is invariant along every edge of `G.deleteEdges {s(u, v)}`: such an edge is `G.Adj x y` with
   `s(x, y) ≠ s(u, v)`, so by `hadj` either `par y = x` or `par x = y`. If `par x = y` and
   `par^[k] x = v`, then `par^[k+1] y = v`. If `par y = x` and `par^[k] x = v`, then `k = 0` would
   give `x = v` and `y = par v = u`, i.e. the deleted edge — excluded; so `k ≥ 1` and
   `par^[k-1] y = par^[k-1] (par y)`… reassociating, `par^[k] y = par^[k-1] x`'s predecessor chain
   reaches `v`. Either way `Anc y`.
3. `¬ Anc u`: `Anc u` means `par^[k] (par v) = v`, i.e. `par^[k+1] v = v`; `hle` iterated gives
   `rk (par^[k+1] v) ≤ rk (par v) < rk v`, contradicting `rk v = rk (par^[k+1] v)`.

A walk from `u` to `v` in the deleted graph would carry `Anc u` from `Anc v` by (2) along its reverse,
contradicting (3). ∎

This is a clean general statement about rooted forests and is a natural `FormalSystem/ForMathlib/`
citizen (existing layout: `ForMathlib/{Order,Combinatorics,Topology}/…`, so
`ForMathlib/Combinatorics/SimpleGraphAcyclic.lean`). **Placement is a judgment call, not a
correctness question**: keeping it inside `UniversalCover.lean` honours the task's "new module"
scope, while `ForMathlib/` honours the upstreaming convention and the fact that the lemma mentions
nothing from `FormalSystem.*`. Recommendation: `ForMathlib/`, since `ForMathlib/` imports nothing
from `FormalSystem.*` and this lemma does not either.

#### New content item B — the cover's double seriality and the two one-step lifts.

All three come from a single case analysis, and they are what make `TwoWayBounded` work. Given
`x : CoverPt E` with `x.endpt = a`:

- **Forward lift.** Given `E a v`, produce `y` with `CoverStep E x y` and `y.endpt = v`. If
  `x.last ≠ some (false, v)`, take `y := x` extended by `fwd` (admitted by that very hypothesis). If
  `x.last = some (false, v)`, then `x` was built by `bwd` from a walk ending at `v`, so
  `CoverStep E x x.par` holds by `down` and `x.par.endpt = v`.
- **Backward lift.** Given `E u a`, produce `y` with `CoverStep E y x` and `y.endpt = u`. If
  `x.last ≠ some (true, u)`, take `y := x` extended by `bwd`. If `x.last = some (true, u)`, then
  `CoverStep E x.par x` holds by `up` and `x.par.endpt = u`.
- **Double seriality** is the two lifts fed by the base's `fwd`/`bwd` (which are free from
  `[F.IsRegular]`, **F3**).

*This is the mathematical heart of the task and the place where "directed walks are reduced paths"
becomes a theorem rather than a gloss: the lift is total precisely because the one move the
reducedness rule forbids is the one move whose target is already a cover-neighbour.*

Iterate to the `iter` level by induction on `n`, using `iter`'s left-recursive shape
(`iter R (n+1) w u ↔ ∃ v, iter R n w v ∧ R v u`, `IntNormalForm.lean:138`), generalizing over the
cover point:

```lean
theorem endpt_iter {n} {x y : CoverPt E} : iter (CoverStep E) n x y → iter E n x.endpt y.endpt
theorem exists_iter_lift_fwd {n} (x : CoverPt E) {v} (h : iter E n x.endpt v) :
    ∃ y, iter (CoverStep E) n x y ∧ y.endpt = v
theorem exists_iter_lift_bwd {n} (x : CoverPt E) {u} (h : iter E n u x.endpt) :
    ∃ y, iter (CoverStep E) n y x ∧ y.endpt = u
```

The backward one inducts with the one-step lift applied **first** (at `x`) and the induction
hypothesis **second** (at the lifted predecessor), which is the order `iter`'s shape forces.

## Decisions

1. **The Quiver route is rejected**, with reasons recorded at **F5** and summarized at Decision 1.
   The task description names `Mathlib/Combinatorics/Quiver/Covering.lean` as "the recorded starting
   point"; research establishes that it defines only the covering *property*, constructs nothing, and
   would leave the `acyclic` field of `IsOrientedForest` entirely unpaid. This is a deliberate
   departure from the task text's recorded starting point and is called out as such.
2. **Base points are taken over all of `W`**, not one per connected component (Decision 2). Flagged
   as a presentational deviation from report 03 §1.2 in §Literature Proof Structure.
3. **Reducedness is structural**, carried by an `Option (Bool × W)` index recording the last move's
   direction and pre-vertex (Decision 3). The alternative — a flat `List (Bool × W)` with `Valid`
   and `Reduced` side predicates — was considered and set aside: its reducedness predicate is a
   three-element sliding window over the vertex sequence (it depends on the vertex *before* the
   previous move, hence on the origin at the first step), which is materially more awkward than the
   index.
4. **The cover's one-step relation is an inductive**, not an equation between `CoverPt`s, to keep
   `HEq` out of every subsequent proof (Decision 4).
5. **`FrameOver.ofStep` is generalized in place** to a `Saturation`-parameterised `ofStepSat`, rather
   than duplicating its 30-line body inside the new module (**F2**). This puts a second file,
   `FormalSystem/Semantics/IntNormalForm.lean`, inside the task's territory.
6. **No `hgen` hypothesis and no "doubly serial base" hypothesis are taken**: both are projections of
   `[F.IsRegular]` over `intOrder` (**F3**).
7. **Unique path lifting is not stated.** It is not consumed by `plus_invariance_of_isZTime` (**F4**),
   and stating it would cost the covering-property machinery for nothing. If a later consumer needs
   it, it is `Prefunctor.IsCovering`-shaped and can be added then.
8. **The acyclicity criterion is stated generally and placed in `ForMathlib/`** (new content item A),
   because it mentions nothing from `FormalSystem.*` and Mathlib has no equivalent (**F6**).

## Risks & Mitigations

**R1 — `(SimpleGraph.fromRel (CoverStep E)).IsAcyclic` is the only genuinely hard obligation.**
*Risk*: Mathlib has no construction-side acyclicity lemma, and a direct assault on `IsAcyclic`'s
definition needs cycle-degree facts about `Walk.IsCycle` that are fiddly at this pin.
*Mitigation*: the bridge-plus-ancestor-invariant proof in **F7** never mentions `Walk.IsCycle`; it
uses `isAcyclic_iff_forall_adj_isBridge` and `isBridge_iff` (which is `.rfl`) and reduces everything
to a reachability invariant proved by walk induction. *Fallback if the invariant proof stalls*: the
same criterion is provable by taking a length-maximal vertex of a cycle's support and deriving two
distinct down-edges; that route is strictly harder but independent, so the obligation has two
routes and neither needs a `sorry`.

**R2 — `[Finite W]` on `FrameOver.ofStep`.** *Risk*: the cover frame cannot be presented at all
without new API, and a naive workaround (copying `ofStep`'s body into the new module) duplicates
`comp`/`serial`/`limit` proofs that would then drift. *Mitigation*: the generalization at **F2** is
one-for-one, keeps `ofStep`'s signature and all three of its existing bridge lemmas, and leaves
every current consumer untouched. *Residual*: it widens the task's file territory to
`IntNormalForm.lean`; the plan should say so and keep the edit minimal.

**R3 — `SuccOrder ↑intOrder` does not synthesise.** *Risk*: the final application of
`plus_invariance_of_isZTime` at `D = intOrder` will fail instance synthesis with a confusing error.
*Mitigation*: this is a known, recorded gate. `FormalSystem/Semantics/ShiftSet.lean:485` documents it
("instance search runs at reducible transparency and `intOrder.carrier` is not *syntactically* `ℤ`
for the discrimination tree, even though `(↑intOrder : Type) = ℤ` holds by `rfl`") and gives the
workaround used twice in the tree: pass the instances positionally with `@` and `inferInstanceAs`,
as `ShiftSet.ofIntAction` (`:494`) and `WitnessFamily.std_isZTime` do. `import Mathlib.Data.Int.SuccPred`
is load-bearing. `IsSuccArchimedean ℤ` will need the same treatment.

**R4 — `Cover` is `Type`-valued and its constructors carry `Prop` side conditions.** *Risk*:
equality of `CoverPt`s and `cases` on `CoverStep` could become awkward if the side conditions were
`Type`-valued or if the indices were not fully determined. *Mitigation*: `hred : m ≠ some (…)` is a
`Prop`, so proof irrelevance makes two `Cover`s equal as soon as their constructor shapes agree;
and both `CoverStep` constructors determine all three indices of both endpoints, so `cases` leaves
no stray index equations. The structural lemma at Decision 5 is designed to be the *only* place
`cases` on `CoverStep` is needed.

**R5 — Phase sizing.** *Risk*: the module has five distinct layers (carrier; structural lemma +
`IsOrientedForest`; seriality + lifts; `iter` lifts + `TwoWayBounded`; assembly) plus the
`ForMathlib` lemma plus the `IntNormalForm` generalization; attempting them in one dispatch invites
a mid-phase stall with a half-written file. *Mitigation*: the plan should carve them into separate
phases in that order, with the `ForMathlib` criterion and the `ofStepSat` generalization as the first
two (both are independent of the carrier and each is verifiable on its own), so that every phase
ends on a green scoped build.

**R6 — Zero-debt screening, explicit.** No step above is discharged by a placeholder. In particular:
no `sorry` is proposed anywhere, no axiom is introduced, no `def … := True`/`Unit`/`trivial`
appears, and no `Decidable`-valued declaration is proposed at all (so the `noncomputable`
`Decidable` vacuity trap is not in play). The construction introduces no `Classical.choice` of its
own — Decision 2 removes the base-point choice, and `saturation_of_orientedForest` is the choice-free
route to *Saturation* rather than `saturation_of_finite`. **If the acyclicity obligation (R1) were to
resist both routes**, the correct response is to mark the phase `[BLOCKED]` with the reached goal
state recorded, not to weaken `IsOrientedForest.acyclic` to directed-path uniqueness — the archived
amendment to report 03 §1.2 proves that the weaker hypothesis does not discharge what the consumer
consumes.
